Accuracy

Float functions give results within 4 units in the last place (ulps) of the correctly rounded result, apart from the exceptions below. Where rustymath gives up a few ulps for real speed or a simpler implementation, it lists the loss here.

Exceptions

  • lgamma near its zeros at 1 and 2 is within 4 ulps of 2, not of the result. digamma near its zeros is within 4 ulps of 1, and below -20 within 4 ulps of ln(1 - x).
  • binom and binom_dn of a non-whole n with a whole k up to 64 are within 2k ulps.
  • binom_dk has no relative bound near n = 2k, where its two ψ terms cancel.
  • beta is within 6 ulps at f64: two of its three ln Γ pair up as one difference, and the third carries the logarithm of its Lanczos sum in floats.
  • pow by a whole exponent from -8 to 8, or by -1.5, squares and multiplies in single words. A rounding at the step that gives x^k carries into x^m about m/k times. The worst errors measured over every f32 significand and a million random f64 ones are 4.4 ulps for x^7, 5.2 for x^8, 4.1 for x^-5, 5.2 for x^-6, 5.7 for x^-7, 7.1 for x^-8 and 4.4 for 1/√x^3. Where x^m overflows, x^-m is 0, though the true result is subnormal: for |x| from 2^(1024/m) to 2^(1075/m) at f64, and from 2^(128/m) to 2^(150/m) at f32, for m from 2 to 8 and for m = 1.5.
  • Normal and log-normal evaluation of f32 inputs uses f32 arithmetic, with parameters and precomputed coefficients rounded to f32. Their densities are within 12 ulps and their cdfs within 14 where |z| < 3, z being the standard score of x or of ln x. They lose accuracy as z² grows, to about 80 and 170 ulps at |z| = 12. Their quantiles are within 13 ulps, except near a quantile of 0, where cancellation leaves an absolute error of up to 8 ulps of the standard deviation. These figures exclude additional error from parameter rounding and ill-conditioned location/scale transformations.
  • Uniform, exponential, Cauchy, Laplace, logistic and Weibull evaluation of f32 inputs uses f32 arithmetic too, with parameters and precomputed coefficients rounded to f32. At both widths their densities and cdfs are within 4 ulps, with two exceptions. The logistic density at f64 is within 4.4 ulps, from the rounding of its exponential, square and quotient. The Weibull density loses accuracy as z^shape grows, z being x/scale: within 4 ulps up to z^shape = 4, then about z^shape ulps, such as 15, 50 and 240 at 16, 64 and 256. Rounding z^shape into the exponent of e(-zshape) costs that much, whatever computes the power. Their quantiles are within 4 ulps, except near a quantile of 0 for the Cauchy, Laplace and logistic laws, where cancellation in location + scale·l leaves an absolute error of up to 4 ulps of the scale. These figures exclude additional error from parameter rounding, ill-conditioned location/scale transformations, and arguments whose standardized value is subnormal.
  • Complex functions count ulps of the result’s magnitude, so a much smaller part can carry most of the error. Complex powers, Γ and binomial coefficients allow ulps of their magnitude times one more than the exponent’s magnitude. exp gives an exact direction at the float nearest a whole number of quarter turns, so e^(iπ/2) is exactly i.

Not promised

  • Last bits. A result’s last bits can differ between thread counts, array sizes, platforms, builds and runs. A fold such as a sum adds in an order that depends on these.
  • NaN behaviour. Where an argument is NaN, a function can give NaN or, as max and min can, another argument. Which it gives isn’t promised, nor which NaN it gives, with which sign and payload. These can differ by function, route and platform.
  • The sign of zero. A zero result can be +0 or -0, which can differ by function, route and platform. Today sign returns a zero argument unchanged. Floor and ceiling give +0 for any zero result.
